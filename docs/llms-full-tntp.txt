tags: [k-12]
```

The pandemic widened achievement and opportunity gaps, and students who were furthest behind have had the hardest time recovering. Incoherent instruction across core classes, intervention and other academic supports slows learning acceleration and deepens those gaps. This TNTP publication argues that districts can change students' experiences and results by aligning instruction, content and goals in every learning setting, so that all students can take part in grade-level work. Using national data and district examples, it illustrates coherence in classrooms and across systems, explains its importance for acceleration, and how coherent academic supports help educators make better decisions for students.

Teachers shape students' academic success as well as their later well-being and economic mobility, and one in 12 college-educated U.S. workers is a teacher. Research identifies teacher quality as the most important school-based factor in students' motivation, achievement and later outcomes, with effects that are immediate and lasting. TNTP's Paths of Opportunity found that young people with strong academic experiences who do well in school are likelier as adults to report high well-being and to earn a living wage, and economists estimate that a single year with an excellent rather than an average teacher adds thousands of dollars to lifetime earnings.

```

The Irreplaceables described a retention crisis in urban schools, which neither hold on to their most effective teachers nor move out ineffective ones. This case study looks at how those retention strategies are working in District of Columbia Public Schools (DCPS), which has made retention smarter chiefly through higher expectations and the removal of teachers who keep performing poorly, and where more progress is possible.
