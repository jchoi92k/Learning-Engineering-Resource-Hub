HSLS:09 is a nationally representative longitudinal study of more than 23,000 9th graders from 944 schools in 2009, with follow-ups in 2012 and 2016 and postsecondary administrative records through 2021. The study includes surveys of students, parents, math and science teachers, school administrators, and counselors, along with a student assessment in algebraic skills, reasoning, and problem solving. Data collection waves span from the 2009 base year through postsecondary transcripts, enabling research on transitions from high school into college and the workforce, including 10 state-representative datasets.

ELS:2002 is a nationally representative longitudinal study of 10th graders in 2002, following students through secondary and postsecondary years with data collection waves spanning from the 2002 base year through a 2012 third follow-up and 2013 postsecondary transcripts. ```

The National Teacher and Principal Survey (NTPS) is a system of related questionnaires that provides descriptive data on the context of public and private elementary and secondary education in the United States. Redesigned from the Schools and Staffing Survey (SASS), NTPS collects information directly from teachers and principals to provide local, state, and national policymakers with statistics on the condition of education. The survey supports analysis of principal, teacher, and student characteristics in detail and enables subgroup analysis (e.g., by gender, race/ethnicity, school locale). Paired with Teacher Follow-up Surveys (TFS) and Principal Follow-up Surveys (PFS), NTPS tracks teacher and principal attrition and mobility. The most recent main collection covered 2020–21, with follow-up surveys in 2021–22.

The National Assessment of Adult Literacy (NAAL) is a comprehensive national assessment measuring English language literacy skills among American adults aged 16 and older, representing the nation's most comprehensive measure of adult literacy since the 1992 National Adult Literacy Survey. The 2003 survey involved over 19,000 participants across all 50 states and D.C., plus approximately 1,200 incarcerated individuals in federal and state prisons. The Schools and Staffing Survey (SASS) was an integrated national study of public and private school districts, schools, principals, and teachers designed to provide descriptive data on the context of elementary and secondary education. The Private School Universe Survey (PSS) generates biennial data on private schools, teachers, and students in the United States, targeting all institutions not primarily supported by public funds that provide classroom instruction for grades K–12 and employ at least one teacher. ```

The National Longitudinal Study of the High School Class of 1972 (NLS-72) pioneered longitudinal research methodology for examining how high school experiences predict career and educational outcomes, following over 21,000 high school seniors through periodic surveys until 1986, with postsecondary transcripts collected in 1984. The study intentionally oversampled schools serving disadvantaged and minority populations and linked educational experiences with early labor market participation and postsecondary enrollment. Findings include that over 90% of participants engaged in paid work within two years of graduation, and the study established a foundational design replicated in subsequent NCES longitudinal programs.


The High School Transcript Studies (HST) collect official academic records from high school students to examine coursetaking patterns and predict future educational outcomes, gathering data on courses taken, credits earned, course sequencing, final grades, class rank, and standardized test scores where available. Transcript data were collected in conjunction with multiple NCES longitudinal studies (HS&B, NELS:88, ELS:2002, HSLS:09) and the National Assessment of Educational Progress (NAEP), spanning collection years from 1982 to 2019. The Current Population Survey (CPS) is a monthly household survey of approximately 50,000–60,000 dwelling units conducted by the Bureau of the Census for the Bureau of Labor Statistics, with NCES funding educational supplements since the late 1960s to provide annual data on school enrollment and educational attainment. The survey's October Supplement gathers school enrollment data across elementary, secondary, and postsecondary levels, plus preschool participation, with supplementary items covering language proficiency, disabilities, computer use and access, student mobility, and private school tuition. The CPS employs a multistage stratified sampling scheme with households rotating in-sample for four months, out for eight, and back for four more, producing nationally representative estimates of the civilian noninstitutionalized population.
